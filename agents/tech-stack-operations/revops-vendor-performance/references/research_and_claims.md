# Research And Claim Discipline

## Current primary sources

1. NIST, *Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management Practices for Systems and Organizations*, SP 800-161 Rev. 1 Update 1, final 2024: https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.800-161r1-upd1
2. NIST, *Cybersecurity Framework 2.0: Quick-Start Guide for Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management*, SP 1305, final 2024: https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.1305
3. NIST, *Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management: Due Diligence Assessment Quick-Start Guide*, SP 1326, final 2026-07-08: https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/1326/final
4. NIST CSF 2.0 Core/Informative Reference filters for GV.SC supplier governance and monitoring: https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/cybersecurity-framework/filters
5. CISA, *Vendor Supply Chain Risk Management Template*: https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/operationalizing-vendor-scrm-template-smbs

## What these sources support

They support lifecycle supplier-risk management, named supplier requirements, due diligence, evidence collection, roles, ongoing monitoring, incident coordination, and transition planning. They do not validate a generic vendor-performance score, universal weights, RevOps renewal thresholds, market-price benchmark, or automatic action.

## Quarantined inherited claims

Do not repeat the legacy package's labor-cost ranges, missed-credit ranges, annual price increase, market medians, ticket-count thresholds, adoption thresholds, roadmap percentages, ROI, savings, or “healthy/at-risk/failing” bands without current evidence tied to the exact population and decision.

Do not cite vendor marketing, status pages, public roadmaps, questionnaires, or self-attestations as independent evidence. Label them `VENDOR_ATTESTED`.

## Claim labels

Use `VERIFIED_OBSERVATION`, `CONTRACTUAL_REQUIREMENT`, `VENDOR_ATTESTED`, `INDEPENDENT_EVIDENCE`, `INFERENCE`, `UNKNOWN`, and `CONFLICT`. Link every material claim to evidence IDs.

If a source does not prove the claim, narrow the claim or return unavailable.
