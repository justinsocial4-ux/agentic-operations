# Security, Privacy, And Supplier Due Diligence

## Boundaries

This skill can organize evidence; it cannot certify security, privacy, compliance, legality, or acceptable risk. Use an authorized security/privacy/procurement reviewer for conclusions.

## Evidence fields

For questionnaires, audits, certifications, attestations, penetration summaries, privacy terms, incident reports, and insurance records, capture issuer, subject, scope, criteria/version, issue and expiry dates, exceptions, remediation status, reliance limits, evidence kind, and reviewer.

A certification name without scope and dates is `INDETERMINATE`. A vendor questionnaire is `VENDOR_ATTESTED`. Independent audit evidence remains bounded to its stated scope and period.

## NIST and CISA routing

- NIST SP 800-161 Rev. 1 Update 1 frames cybersecurity supply-chain risk as a lifecycle practice of identifying, assessing, and mitigating risk.
- NIST SP 1305 uses CSF 2.0 to operate C-SCRM and define/communicate supplier requirements.
- NIST CSF 2.0 GV.SC calls for known suppliers prioritized by criticality, documented roles, contractual requirements, due diligence, ongoing monitoring, incident coordination, and transition planning.
- NIST SP 1326 (final 2026-07-08) describes minimum investigative due diligence for ICT suppliers, including supply-chain tiers, foreign ownership/control/influence, provenance, stability, and foundational cyber practices.
- CISA's Vendor SCRM template offers standardized supplier questions and `Yes`/`No`/`Partial` explanations.

These sources guide evidence structure. They do not make this RevOps review a complete NIST assessment or make vendor answers independently verified.

## Criticality

Use a customer-approved criticality policy based on factors such as service importance, data sensitivity, access, concentration, and recovery dependency. Do not create a generic risk score or claim a universal threshold.
