# Workforce, privacy, and accessibility

Territory allocation can affect opportunity, workload, quota, pay, performance evaluation, and employment conditions. Treat it as a consequential workforce surface even when the output is called a recommendation.

Require approved purpose, authorized viewers, data minimization, retention, correction/appeal path, affected-party review, accessibility review, labor/works-council review where applicable, and a named human approver. Use pseudonymous rep IDs and approved work anchors.

Exclude names, emails, free text, home locations, protected traits, manager narratives, content, and historical performance/discipline/quota signals by default. Use the exact opaque rep-ID and population-bound pseudonymization-receipt contract. Reviewer, approver, owner, correction, and policy fields use stable role or receipt IDs, never contact details or narrative. Do not infer availability, capacity, commitment, suitability, accommodation needs, or attrition risk.

NIST AI RMF calls for explicit roles, oversight, impact mapping, and feedback: https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/ . The EEOC identifies algorithmic employment decisions as a discrimination risk surface: https://www.eeoc.gov/ai . These sources support governance, not a legal-compliance verdict.
